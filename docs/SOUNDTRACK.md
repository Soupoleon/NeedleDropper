# Azeroth Unbound: liner notes

*The optional Kojima-style soundtrack, built into NeedleDropper* (`packs/azeroth-unbound.json`).

In Death Stranding, Hideo Kojima saves his licensed songs for one moment: you crest a ridge after a
long walk, the landscape opens up, and a melancholy, spacious song starts as the title card fades in.
The music makes you feel small, far from home and quietly hopeful. *Azeroth Unbound* tries to do the
same for World of Warcraft: Forever. The first time a character arrives somewhere, it plays one song
chosen for that place, then lets the world go quiet again.

NeedleDropper starts you on [*Azeroth Mixtape*](MIXTAPE.md), the iconic places with fitting and
funny songs. *Azeroth Unbound* is the other built-in soundtrack: the quiet score for every zone. To
turn it on, type `/needle`, open the **Soundtracks** tab and tick **On** next to *Azeroth Unbound*.
Keep both on, with the Mixtape above it, and the Mixtape plays at its 38 places while Unbound scores
everywhere else; or turn the Mixtape off for the pure, unbroken Kojima mood.

The soundtrack has **62 drops**: 50 zones and capitals (every open-world zone in the
Forever map table, battlegrounds excluded) and 12 landmark arrivals, by **47 artists**.
Low Roar, whose songs carry Death Stranding, is the spiritual anchor and appears a few times. A handful
of other Death Stranding songs land where they genuinely fit. Everything else is a new pick in the
same spirit: post-rock, indie folk, modern classical and ambient electronica.

A needle drop is an arrival, not a concert. Most songs play in full; the five that run past seven
minutes play as 6:30 excerpts, and the notes below give their full length.

The soundtrack holds **metadata only**: no audio and no lyrics. The NeedleDropper app plays each song
through your own Spotify Premium account (it looks the song up by artist and title as you approach the
place) or through YouTube, in a small player window that stays visible while the song plays.
**Every drop except Azshara has a YouTube version**: an official upload of the same recording, from the
artist's or label's channel or YouTube's auto-generated "Topic" upload. Azshara (M83, "Wait") has none
yet: M83's Topic uploads ask viewers to sign in to YouTube, and the official video runs longer than the
album track, so that drop plays through Spotify, or through an audio file of your own if you give it
one (see [Make it yours](#make-it-yours)).

Each landmark carries its area id as well as its English name, so it triggers, and its title card
shows the right place name, on every game language.

## Kalimdor

### Durotar

**Bon Iver, "The Wolves (Act I and II)"** (5:22)

Red rock, hot wind and a people newly freed: a hushed, slowly swelling lament for wolf riders who are only now learning what home means.

### Mulgore

**Novo Amor, "Birthplace"** (3:10)

The tauren's cradle of golden grass and slow rivers deserves a song literally named for where you were born.

#### Skywatcher Plateau (Mulgore)

**Tycho, "Awake"** (4:43)

A new lookout high above the plains of Mulgore, named for watching the sky: warm, wide-open sunlight in song form.

### The Barrens

**Explosions in the Sky, "Your Hand in Mine"** (6:30 excerpt of 8:17)

An endless savanna under a huge sky; the guitars build and recede like heat over the road to the Crossroads.

### Teldrassil

**Ólafur Arnalds, "Near Light"** (3:28)

Moonlight filtered through the World Tree's canopy: piano, strings and a soft electronic pulse that glow rather than shine.

### Darkshore

**Sigur Rós, "Sæglópur"** (6:30 excerpt of 7:38)

The title means 'lost at sea', and Darkshore is a grey, mournful coast full of shipwrecks and drowned ruins.

### Ashenvale

**The Album Leaf, "The Light"** (4:29)

Ancient twilight forest where shafts of light still break through the canopy, even as the axes of war bite at its edge.

### Thousand Needles

**Silent Poets, "Asylums for the Feeling"** (6:30 excerpt of 7:09)

A Death Stranding needle drop (with Leila Adu on vocals) for a landscape that could be from it: towering mesas, a long way down, and a lot of quiet.

### Stonetalon Mountains

**Hania Rani, "Esja"** (4:21)

Named for a mountain over Reykjavík; climbing piano for the high, wind-cut peaks above the logging scars.

### Desolace

**Low Roar, "Don't Be So Serious"** (6:12)

A grey, bone-strewn wasteland of kodo graveyards and warring centaur, scored by Low Roar at their most weary and tender.

### Feralas

**Sufjan Stevens, "Mystery of Love"** (4:08)

Humid summer light, overgrown elven ruins and giant trees: a lush, aching song for a forgotten corner of the world.

### Dustwallow Marsh

**Radiohead, "Daydreaming"** (6:24)

Fog over black water and a city at the edge of everything; this song drifts and loops like the marsh itself.

### Tanaris

**Nils Frahm, "A Shimmer"** (6:36)

Dunes wavering in the heat haze, with the Caverns of Time somewhere beyond them: a spare upright-piano piece whose title says the rest.

#### Gadgetzan (Tanaris)

**Khruangbin, "Maria También"** (3:10)

A walled goblin market in the middle of the desert, scored with a sun-baked, bazaar-flavoured groove.

### Azshara

**M83, "Wait"** (5:43)

An eternal autumn coast of ruined Highborne palaces; a gentle, dream-heavy song about a glamour that is already gone.

### Felwood

**Agnes Obel, "The Curse"** (5:53)

A forest poisoned from the roots up, and a song titled for exactly that.

### Un'Goro Crater

**Jon Hopkins, "Emerald Rush"** (5:36)

Primeval green, hot springs and dinosaurs: a pulsing, overgrown rush of a track for the crater floor.

#### Marshal's Refuge (Un'Goro Crater)

**The Cinematic Orchestra, "To Build a Home"** (6:11)

A camp clinging to a cliff in a prehistoric world: a song about the fragile shelters we make.

### Moonglade

**Brian Eno, "An Ending (Ascent)"** (4:24)

The druids' sanctuary is the one place no war reaches; Eno's slow ascent is pure stillness.

#### Nighthaven (Moonglade)

**Nick Drake, "Northern Sky"** (3:43)

The druid village by the moonlit lake, greeted with a warm, glowing love song under a northern sky.

### Silithus

**Jóhann Jóhannsson, "Heptapod B"** (3:42)

Something alien lives under these dunes; Jóhannsson's choir-and-drone piece from Arrival makes the hives feel vast and unknowable.

#### Cenarion Hold (Silithus)

**Low Roar, "Easy Way Out"** (4:48)

A lonely druid outpost holding the line against the swarm; Low Roar's weary hope for people who refuse to leave.

### Winterspring

**Bon Iver, "Holocene"** (5:36)

Snowfields so wide they make you feel small, which is exactly what this song is about.

#### Everlook (Winterspring)

**Fleet Foxes, "White Winter Hymnal"** (2:27)

A warm goblin town in the snowfields, greeted by a round of voices singing about winter.

### Orgrimmar

**Bring Me The Horizon, "Ludens"** (4:38)

Iron spikes, war drums and a city built in defiance; the loudest Death Stranding song for the loudest capital.

### Thunder Bluff

**Jónsi, "Go Do"** (4:41)

Drums, flutes and wind high on the mesas: a joyful, primal call that fits the tauren's city above the clouds.

### Darnassus

**AURORA, "Runaway"** (4:08)

A temple city of moonwells and owls; an ethereal Nordic song about longing for a home far away.

### Mount Hyjal

**M83, "Outro"** (4:07)

The climb toward Nordrassil, where the world was once saved, earns the biggest swell in the pack.

### Shen'dralas

**Woodkid, "To the Wilder"** (4:39)

A brand-new land of Highborne exiles and centaur plains, scored with a Death Stranding 2 song about heading out into the unknown.

The city of mages sealed under a violet dome, seen from outside like something behind glass.

## Make it yours

*Azeroth Unbound* is built in and read-only, but you can change any of its songs in game. Type
`/needle` to open the NeedleDropper window, and pick *Azeroth Unbound* under **Showing:** on the
**Drops** tab to list its 62 drops.

1. **Find the place.** Search for it (*Duskwood*, *Low Roar*), or go there: the window opens on the
   drop for where you stand.
2. **Change the song.** Click **Change song here**. NeedleDropper copies the drop into **My Drops**,
   your own soundtrack at the top of your list, and puts the cursor in **Artist**. Type the new artist
   and title, and paste a Spotify or YouTube link to pin the exact recording. Under **More…**,
   **Play for** turns a long song into an excerpt (as this soundtrack does for its five longest songs)
   and **Start at** skips an intro. ▶ **Test** plays it at once.
3. **Use your own audio file.** Copy an .mp3 or .ogg into
   `World of Warcraft\_forever_\Interface\AddOns\NeedleDropper_Local\media`, restart the game (WoW
   only notices new files when it starts), and type the file's name into the drop's **In-game file**
   field. WoW then plays it itself, with no app needed. That is also the way to give Azshara a song
   without Spotify.
4. **Or silence a place:** **Turn off here**. **Revert to Azeroth Unbound** undoes either change.
5. **Add landmarks it doesn't cover:** stand inside one and press **Add drop here**.

Changes apply at once, with no `/reload`, and later fixes to Azeroth Unbound still reach every place
you didn't change. For a full copy to rework from top to bottom, use **Duplicate** on the
**Soundtracks** tab; share your version with **Share…** there.

Drops play once per character. To hear a place's first-arrival moment again, click **Play again next
visit** (or ▶ **Replay**) on the **Journal** tab, type `/needle play Duskwood` to hear any drop now,
or `/needle reset char` to start this character over. If you played NeedleDropper 1.1, this was its
soundtrack: the places you heard then stay heard. The file format for soundtrack authors is in
[Soundtracks](PACKS.md#for-soundtrack-authors-the-pack-format).
